Headline

Recruiter's 3-second reaction: "Another CS student. Next."
Problem

Your headline reads: "B.Tech CSE Student | Full Stack Developer | MERN Stack | AI & Web Development" This is four labels stacked with pipes, not a positioning statement. "AI & Web Development" is especially vague — it tells recruiters nothing about what you actually build.

Why it hurts

Recruiters search LinkedIn using specific keywords tied to roles ("Frontend Developer," "React Developer," "MERN Stack Developer") and outcomes. A list of nouns with no verb, no specialization, and no proof reads as a template every other CS student is using — because it is.

Invisible cost

You're not ranking for the searches that matter, and even when someone lands on your profile, nothing here differentiates you from thousands of identical headlines. This is the single highest-leverage line on your entire profile, and right now it's doing zero work.

About — First 2 Lines

Recruiter's 3-second reaction: There's nothing to react to — it's empty.
Problem

You have no About section at all.

Why it hurts

The first two lines of your About section are the most valuable real estate on your profile — they show before "see more." Recruiters and hiring managers decide whether to keep reading in that window. An empty section isn't neutral; it actively signals low effort or an inactive profile.

Invisible cost

You're skipping the one section where you get to control the narrative in your own words — not a job title, not a bullet point, an actual pitch. Every day this stays blank is a day recruiters bounce off your profile without learning anything about who you are.

About — Full Section

Recruiter's 3-second reaction: N/A — nothing to read.
Problem

Same issue — this section doesn't exist yet.

Why it hurts

This is where you're supposed to answer: what do you do, who do you help, why does it matter, and what's your proof? Without it, your profile is just a list of facts (title, one internship, some skills) with no story connecting them.

Invisible cost

Recruiters filling entry-level roles are often forgiving on experience but not on clarity of intent. Without an About section, you look unclear about your own direction — even if that's not true.

4

Comment on 5 posts in your target space

▶

The Value Comment formula: Agree + Add Insight + Ask a Question.

1. Agree — one line acknowledging their actual point (not "Great post!").
2. Add insight — a specific example, counterpoint, or thing you've observed/tried yourself.
3. Ask a question — invites a reply, which is what gets you noticed by the poster and their audience.

Example: "Agree that state management gets overused early on — I ran into this building a Hospital Management System, where local component state was genuinely enough for most of the UI. Curious where you'd draw the line for when Redux actually earns its complexity?"

Target posts from the recruiters/engineers you just connected with, plus 2-3 well-known devs in the MERN/React space.

5

Publish Post #2 — lessons-learned / hot take

▶
Building a Hospital Management System solo taught me something no tutorial did: the hardest part of full-stack dev isn't React or Node — it's the data model. I redesigned my MongoDB schema for patients and appointments three times before it actually held up against real edge cases (double-bookings, doctors with overlapping schedules, cancellations). Tutorials show you the happy path. Real projects show you why the happy path was never the whole story. If you're a student building your first full project — spend more time on your data model than you think you need to. It'll save you from rebuilding your frontend logic later. What's a "small" decision on one of your projects that ended up being the hardest part?
