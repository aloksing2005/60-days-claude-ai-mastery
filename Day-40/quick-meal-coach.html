Role & scope

The system prompt casts Claude as a friendly, encouraging nutrition-minded home cook — not a doctor and not a generic chatbot. Its only job is to turn "here's what I have and how I feel" into 2–3 cookable recipes that respect the stated time limit, diet, and goal. Anything outside that (medical advice, meal plans spanning days, general chit-chat) is explicitly out of scope and the prompt tells Claude to redirect politely rather than improvise.

Structured output contract

Claude is instructed to respond with JSON only, matching a fixed schema (recipe name, time, description, match reasoning, macro estimates, ingredients with amounts, and numbered steps). The UI never parses prose — it parses JSON keys — which is what makes the recipe cards, macro bars, and step list render reliably instead of breaking on whatever phrasing the model chooses that turn.

Edge cases handled in the prompt

  • Missing info: if no ingredients are given, Claude assumes a reasonably stocked home kitchen and says so in the reasoning, instead of stalling.
  • Impossible constraints: if the time/diet/ingredients can't produce a real dish, Claude returns a shorter list (even one option) rather than inventing something that doesn't fit.
  • Irrelevant or abusive input: non-food requests or abuse get a short, calm redirect back to meal planning, still returned as valid JSON so the UI can display it as a message rather than crashing.
  • Medical/allergy claims: Claude respects stated allergies strictly, but adds a disclaimer field encouraging the person to double check labels — it never plays doctor.

Why these UI choices

Busy professionals want to scan, not read paragraphs. Three recipe cards with a time badge, a one-line "why this fits" ribbon, and macro bars let someone compare options in seconds — closer to browsing a menu than reading a chat transcript. Clicking a card expands into a focused ingredient list and numbered steps, mirroring how a recipe card actually gets used at the stove.
